## 1 Introduction

## 3 The targets

### 3.1 Gamma Sagittae

Located in the arrow-shaped constellation Sagitta, Gamma Sagittae is a red giant with a distinct orange hue. At about 260 light years, it marks the shaft of the arrow and is easily visible without optical aid.

### 3.2 Gamma Delphini

A double star in Delphinus, Gamma Delphini is best observed with binoculars or a small telescope. The system consists of a yellow and an orange star, which contrast in colour.

### 3.3 Epsilon Delphini

Epsilon Delphini (Deneb Dulfim) is a blue-white giant and the brightest star of the small constellation Delphinus. About 358 light years from Earth, it marks the tip of the dolphin's tail and is easily seen with the naked eye.

### 3.4 Zeta Aquilae

Part of the constellation Aquila, Zeta Aquilae is a binary system about 83 light years away. To the naked eye it appears as a single star; binoculars reveal a slight difference in colour between its components.

### 3.5 Alpha Aquilae

Alpha Aquilae (Altair) is a white star, the twelfth brightest in the night sky, and one corner of the Summer Triangle. At 17 light years, it is one of the closest bright stars to Earth. It rotates so rapidly that it bulges at its equator.
