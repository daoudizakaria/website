### 1 Introduction

The application of statistical physics to finance has become mandatory for a profound understanding of modern financial analysis \[2]. Using the tools of equilibrium statistical mechanics,we can study the process for the empirical distribution of income and wealth in a closed system.Although this concept was criticized due to the impossibility of considering a closed systemin finance, an attempt has been made by Stefano Viaggiu et al.\[1] to build a formalism where credit is introduced as a new variable in the monetary base, allowing for a more realistic representation of the credit market. In \[3, 4], the framework of structural credit risk is built, and then the authors focus on applying the analytical model to a homogeneous portfolio 1. The goal of this literature review is to introduce physicists to the application of statistical physics in the economy, particularly structural credit risk modeling. It seeks to highlight both the strengths and the ongoing challenges for this type of model.

##### 2.1 Microstates and the Boltzmann Distribution

Consider a system that can exist in a large number of microstates, each characterized by an energy In thermal equilibrium, and setting the Boltzmann constant to unity, the probability that the system occupies a state with an energy is given by the Boltzmann distribution:

$$

P(\epsilon)=\frac{1}{Z}e^{-\frac{\epsilon}{T}},

$$



where \*\*T\*\* is the temperature and \*\*Z\*\* is the partition function defined as



$$

Z=\sum_i e^{-\frac{\epsilon_i}{T}}.

$$



Considering that the sum is taken over all states of the system, the expectation value of any physical variable \(x\) is given by



$$

\langle x\rangle=

\frac{\sum_k x_k e^{-\frac{\epsilon_k}{T}}}

{\sum_k e^{-\frac{\epsilon_k}{T}}}.

$$



\---



####  Entropy and the Maximum Entropy Principle



The entropy \(S\) of the system quantifies the degree of uncertainty or the number of microstates accessible to the system:



$$

S=-\sum_i P_i\ln P_i.

$$



By applying the maximum entropy principle, one obtains the equilibrium probability distribution by maximizing \(S\) subject to the constraints of normalization and a fixed average energy:



Maximize



$$

S=-\sum_i P_i\ln P_i,

$$



subject to



$$

\sum_i P_i=1

$$



and



$$

\sum_i E_iP_i=\langle E\rangle.

$$
